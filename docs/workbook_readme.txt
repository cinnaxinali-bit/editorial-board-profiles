Editorial Review Board scholars: profiles (researched October 2026)
What is in this workbook:
• Scholars: one row per person on the Editorial Review Board list you provided (358 people), in the original alphabetical order (# = position in the list).
• Summary: live counts (formulas) by area and position, standardized subfields, the most common PhD universities, and data-quality counts.
• Filters are on in the Scholars sheet: use the "Strategy & OT vs OB" and "Position" columns to slice the board.
Columns:
• Website (faculty page) = official profile at the current institution; Personal website and CV link are filled only when one was found. Links were taken from search results/pages, not constructed.
• Institution (current) may differ from "Institution as listed on ERB" when the person has moved since the list was published; "Moved since list?" flags these.
• Position = normalized rank (Assistant / Associate / Full / Other); "Exact title" keeps the wording on the official page; "Position note" records promotions, UK/European title equivalences, or conflicts between sources.
• PhD institution / field / year come from faculty bios, CVs and dissertation records. "PhD university (normalized)" merges spelling variants for counting.
How positions were normalized:
• Endowed, chaired or distinguished professors, Hong Kong "Chair Professor", and UK/European "Professor" = Full.
• UK "Associate Professor", "Senior Lecturer" and "Reader" = Associate (noted in Position note).
• UK "Lecturer" and "Assistant Professor" = Assistant.
• Emeritus, teaching-track, administrative-only or non-tenure-line roles = Other (explained in Position note).
How "Strategy & OT vs OB" was assigned:
• OB = primarily micro/meso, psychological research on individuals, dyads and teams (leadership, motivation, emotions, creativity, status and power, negotiation, decision making, ethics, diversity and identity from a psychological lens, well-being).
• Strategy & OT = primarily macro research: strategy, innovation, entrepreneurship, governance, nonmarket strategy, organizational economics, and organization theory / economic sociology (institutions, networks, labor markets and inequality from a sociological lens, qualitative studies of work, occupations and technology).
• Many scholars straddle the line; the label reflects the dominant body of work and home group. "Borderline call?" = Yes marks hybrids, and the "Classification rationale" column explains each call.
• "Subfield" is a standardized category (23 options, used for the Summary counts); "Subfield (detail)" keeps the more specific label from the research notes.
Caveats:
• Titles change every July with promotions and moves; a page that had not been updated could understate a recent promotion. Confidence = high / medium / low summarizes how well sources agreed; low-confidence rows are shaded yellow.
• Research-topic summaries are condensed from each person's own profile, CV and recent papers.
• Sources lists the main URLs consulted for each row.
